**Problem statement (revised)**

Quick-commerce has become a default way people buy groceries, household essentials, snacks, and beverages. Yet most customers shop in a narrow, repeating set of categories. That habit limits cross-category discovery, caps customer lifetime value, and constrains growth for the platform.

Existing recommendations, promotions, and search help surface products, but they rarely address *why* users avoid unfamiliar categories—risk, trust, price uncertainty, weak discovery, or simply habit. At the same time, the evidence that would explain those barriers is scattered across App Store and Play Store reviews, Reddit, social media, forums, and product reviews. Product teams cannot manually synthesize this volume of unstructured feedback into a coherent view of user needs.

As a result, decisions about discovery and assortment often rest on fragmented anecdotes rather than systematic insight. Core questions stay unanswered: Why do users stay within the same categories? What blocks them from trying new ones? How do they discover products today? What information would make them willing to experiment? Which segments are most open to category exploration?

**Opportunity**

An AI-powered discovery insight engine that ingests multi-channel customer feedback at scale, detects recurring themes, sentiments, behavioral patterns, and unmet needs, and turns them into actionable product insights. With that foundation, the platform can improve discovery experiences, encourage cross-category purchase, and increase the share of Monthly Active Customers who explore and buy from at least one new product category each month.
